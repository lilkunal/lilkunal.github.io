## 2. What's the most impactful thing you've built? What was your specific contribution?

The most impactful thing I’ve built and still run is the digital side of **Padma Lights** (padmalights.com) — a cast-aluminium outdoor lighting business with a **200+ product** catalogue, trade pricing, and export orders across **eight currencies**.

My contribution wasn’t a one-week redesign. I set design direction and copy, build with an AI-assisted workflow (Claude/Cursor), then personally review layout, mobile behaviour, and launch quality before anything goes live.

Alongside that, I designed and shipped **JAI Home Care** — a mobile-first landing page for local enquiries — and my own portfolio at **lilkunal.github.io**, including a printable hire sheet. The through-line: marketing web that has to work for real customers, not just look good in a case study.

## 3. How did you know it worked? What did success actually look like?

Success on Padma Lights looks operational, not vanity-metric theatre. The site has to hold up under real buyer behaviour: find a product among 200+, understand trade pricing, and complete an enquiry that can turn into an export order. When that path is clear on phone and desktop, and when currency/pricing presentation doesn’t confuse international buyers, the site is doing its job.

Concretely, success for me meant: fewer “where is this model / what does this cost in my currency?” back-and-forths, catalogue updates shipping without breaking layout, and the business continuing to take digital-led export and trade interest through the site. I treat post-launch support as part of the product — four years in technical support trained me to notice what’s broken for the user, not what’s pretty in Figma.

For client landing pages like JAI Home Care, success is simpler: a visitor on a phone can understand the offer, reach WhatsApp or phone in one or two taps, and the page stays fast and readable. If those paths work without me sitting next to the client, the design worked.

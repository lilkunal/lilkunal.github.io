Three.js, three ways

Crossfade blending and morph targets on a fourteen-clip robot, additive layering on a walk cycle, and a CCD inverse-kinematics arm that reaches for wherever you point. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.

What is actually happening

Crossfade blending
Switching between idle, walk and run does not swap clips. Both play at once while their weights interpolate over 0.4s, so the skeleton never pops between poses.
Additive blending
A second demo. An additive clip stores the difference from its first frame, so a pose like sneak or sad layers on top of the walk instead of replacing it. The pose clips are cut to a single frame first, because a pose is not a movement.
Inverse kinematics
A third demo. CCD walks the arm from the hand back to the shoulder, rotating one joint at a time until the hand reaches its target bone. Switch the target to follow the cursor and the whole arm solves itself as you move.
One-shot with return
Jump, wave and punch play once, clamp on their final frame, then fade back to whichever clip was running before. The listener removes itself so repeated presses do not stack handlers.
Budget
Each demo fetches only its own model, and the DRACO decoder is downloaded only for the compressed one. The renderer stops when the canvas scrolls out of view or the tab is hidden.
